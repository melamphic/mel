Salvia · homepage rework

From a feature list to a story that sells.

The site today shows what the product does. It never says what you lose without it, never frames the agent as someone you'd hire, and the sections don't hand off to each other. This is the arc, drawn from StoryBrand, Julian Shapiro, and teardowns of Vanta, Drata, Secureframe, Cluely and Linear.

The one-line position
Salvia is Vanta for clinical compliance — an always-on agent that keeps every hospital audit-ready, with a clinician's sign-off on every action.
Sell the outcome the administrator loses sleep over — always audit-ready — not the category (“documentation software”). The mechanism lives in the subhead.

The story arc

Customer (quality lead / medical superintendent) is the hero. Salvia is the guide. Every section answers the question the last one raised, and opens the next.
Hero → Frameworks (borrowed credibility) → The problem (you find the gap at the inspection) → The loop (capture→check→seal) → The agent (a compliance officer that never sleeps + you approve everything) → Coverage (know where you stand) → Policy → Registers → The numbers → Trust/data → Close

Copy rules (kill the AI-slop)

›Specific noun + number over adjectives (“PRE.4.a consent”, “62%”, “at submit”).
›One idea per section. Two ideas → split.
›Active, present tense: “the agent flags” not “is designed to flag”.
›A real point of view: “nothing writes to a record without a clinician.”
›No “not X, but Y”, no rule-of-three adjectives, no “in today's landscape”.
›Show, don't claim — real product, real sourced stat, never a fake logo or quote.
