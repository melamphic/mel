A great insurance team is a room full of people you cannot replace.

Everything that makes a claim succeed lives in somebody's head. Which insurer wants what. Which document draws a query. What to do when the diagnosis changes after admission. None of it is written down, and it leaves the building when they do.

01

Most of the process was never online

Insurance in Indian hospitals still runs on paper, scanning, phone calls and email. There is a system of record for the output, and none at all for the work.

02

Nobody sees a gap until it is too late to fix

By the time a query arrives the patient has gone home. Evidence that would have taken thirty seconds on the ward now cannot be created at all.

03

The same work is done twice

Once for the patient, once for the claim. Two departments, two sets of paper, one admission, and no shared view between them.

04

The bed stays blocked

Discharge waits on the file. The file waits on people. The next admission waits on the bed.

Two departments become one desk.

The team that handles inpatients and the team that handles insurance are doing one job in two places. On Salvia they do one job in one place, and most of the work between them stops existing.

Nothing changes on the ward

Your clinical staff keep working exactly as they do today. That is a design constraint, not a nice to have.

Live from the start

You do not wait for the integration to finish before the desk is useful. It works from the first admission.

Built for the national rails

Our claim model follows NHCX's own workflow vocabulary, so the transport is a swap rather than a rebuild.

What Salvia will not do.

Worth writing down, because every other vendor in this market is careful not to.

NEVERUpcode. We will not suggest a diagnosis or a procedure that did not happen, at any margin.
NEVERWrite a clinical note on a clinician's behalf. We show what is missing and ask the person who knows.
NEVERSubmit anything without a person on your team approving it. Autopilot still has a pilot.
NEVERTake a percentage of your claim value. Our incentive should not move with your billing.
NEVERAsk your ward staff to work differently to make our software look good.
NEVERGive a false all clear. If the theatre note never arrived it says not received, never fine.

Show us one week of discharged files.

We will sit with your insurance desk for a day, take the last week of files you have already closed, and show you what was missing and the exact moment it could still have been produced. No slides.

Rules that keep this out of AI slop territory

Motion equals a state changeNothing floats, bobs or shimmers to fill space. If it moves, that thing genuinely happens in the product.
Real product strings onlyIP 2026/4471. Star Health via Medi Assist. Day 4. Implant sticker. All taken from the shipped app, not invented.
One thing moves at a timeInside a frame, one change, then rest. Three simultaneous animations is what makes a page feel generated.
No shadows, no glowHairlines and flat fills only. The gradient field is the single exception and it appears once.
Deep green on light, mint on darkOne hue, two values. Mint is a field or a live signal, never body colour.
Reduced motion is honestprefers-reduced-motion renders the finished frame, not a degraded wobble. Already wired.

Claims audit — before any of this goes public

The award is undocumented. Nothing in the repo holds an organiser announcement, certificate or email for HOSPEX. Keep the proof from Trithvam Integris before the logo goes up, and settle whether the year reads 2026 or 2027.
KSUM and Startup India need checking. The KSUM unique ID is recorded as a to-do, not an achievement, and nothing records a Startup India recognition number. Verify both before those two logo slots are filled.
Everything on the capability list is marked in build, because it is. The claims queue in the app runs on four hard-coded sample claims and sal has no claims endpoints. What is shipped is capture: bedside rounds, outpatients, admissions, vitals, discharge, prescriptions, ABHA.
NHCX wording is deliberately narrow. No date, no "ready", no "certified", no "mandate". Only that the claim model follows NHCX's workflow vocabulary, which is true today.
No hospital names anywhere, no deduction percentage anywhere. Both are barred by the audit. Aster in particular must never appear.
