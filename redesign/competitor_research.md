# Salvia — Competitive & Marketing-Site Research

**Purpose:** Map the competitive landscape for an AI clinical-documentation + agentic-compliance platform, extract the recurring marketing-site patterns, and recommend a site architecture for Salvia (India-first; clinical wedge now, compliance vision layered on).

**Method:** Live homepage/marketing-site fetches (August 2026) across three buckets — global AI scribes, India-specific scribe/compliance/HMIS vendors, and continuous/agentic-compliance (GRC) platforms — plus India accreditation/regulatory context. Hero copy and stats are quoted verbatim from live pages where possible. A few India items (regulatory paragraphs, Practo Ray historical INR pricing) are knowledge-based and flagged.

**Salvia in one line (the thing being positioned):** Clinician records a post-encounter voice note → AI drafts a SOAP note + extracts vitals → typed widgets capture compliance facts → a policy engine checks each note → a self-improving compliance agent continuously maps captured data to the clinic's country/framework requirements (NABH, JCI, NSQHS, CMS/HEDIS). GTM: India first (clinics + hospitals), expanding country-by-country. Trust wedge: pilot doctors buy clinical value (faster/better documentation) first, compliance vision second.

### India accreditation / regulatory context (for credible site copy)
- **NABH** (National Accreditation Board for Hospitals): QCI board that accredits hospitals/clinics/SHCOs against ~100+ standards and ~600+ measurable "objective elements." Hospitals want it because it's tied to **money and market access** — PMJAY empanelment/reimbursement, insurer/TPA preference, tenders, credibility. The pain is the **evidence/documentation burden**: written policies/SOPs, committee minutes, consent + initial-assessment/care-plan completeness in the record, continuous audits (medical-record, medication, hand-hygiene, incident/mortality review), monthly quality indicators — mostly manual, spiking before every assessment/re-accreditation. **This is exactly the pain a compliance-native product removes.**
- **ABDM** (Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, run by NHA): India's health-data interoperability layer — **ABHA** (14-digit patient health ID), HPR/HFR registries, consent-based HIE on FHIR R4. For a vendor, "ABDM compliant" is a concrete **milestone certification** (M1→M2→M3), ABHA verify/create, consent-based record sharing. Voluntary but increasingly a **procurement expectation + trust badge**.
- **DPDP Act 2023** (Digital Personal Data Protection): India's first comprehensive data-protection law. GDPR-adjacent roles (Data Principal / Fiduciary / Processor); consent + notice, purpose limitation, breach notification, retention/erasure; strong child-data rules; **no blanket localization mandate** under the Act itself. Penalties up to **₹250 crore**. For a clinical-AI vendor: consent capture, processor/DPA agreements, breach readiness, and treating audio + transcripts as personal data with defined retention.

### How Indian vendors position differently
- **Compliance badges are local, not Western** — ABDM/ABHA (M1/M2/M3), NHA-Approved, NABH/NABL/JCI carry trust, vs. HIPAA/SOC 2/GDPR + Epic/Cerner in the West. A Western product simply cannot claim ABDM/NABH.
- **Pricing hidden and reframed as affordability** — nearly all are "Book a Demo," no public INR; messaging is mission/affordability, not tiers. Exceptions price in USD (Effivity) or are free (Bahmni). **Public INR tier cards are a genuine gap.**
- **The India wedge is multilingual + voice-to-prescription + registries** — EkaScribe "voice-to-prescription / 15+ Indian languages"; Augnito 37+ languages; value = hours saved + ABHA-linked records. Western scribes optimize SOAP-into-US-EHR with none of these hooks.
- **Compliance is bundled inside HMIS, never sold as the product** — the compliance-native "prove-your-compliance-from-the-clinical-record" position is unoccupied.
- **Trust = vanity scale + marquee hospital logos** (Apollo, Fortis, Manipal, Medanta) rather than analyst reports.

### Continuous/agentic-compliance playbook (transferable to Salvia)
1. **Lead with "trust," relegate "compliance" to the mechanism.** Heroes sell an outcome noun (Trust is everything / Agentic Trust / Build trust) — frameworks appear in the *subheadline* as proof, never the promise.
2. **Make "continuous" tangible via a live-evidence contrast, not an adjective:** "continuous, real-time evidence of your active controls — beyond a one-time snapshot." Pair with a persistent posture score / live dashboard.
3. **Personify the AI as a tireless hire + a trust metric:** give it a role ("the GRC engineer you never had to hire"), a shift ("24/7 / Always on"), and a hard number ("95% acceptance rate").
4. **Three verbs define the agent's job — draft, flag, complete:** "draft policies," "flag issues *before they escalate*," "complete questionnaires." "Second set of eyes" framing keeps the human in the loop.
5. **Quantify everything, sourced to named customers/analysts:** "[X% / Y hours] [saved/faster], per [Snowflake / IDC / Forrester]."
6. **"One platform, many frameworks" = a clickable grid + per-framework landing pages + a "by framework" nav path.**
7. **Ship a Trust Center as both your proof and a product feature** — self-serve posture page, AI chatbot answering from your evidence, auto-approved access.
8. **Pricing posture signals motion:** opacity + named tiers = enterprise/top-down; transparency + free tier (Prowler) = PLG/bottom-up.

**Canonical GRC site architecture:** Platform/Product (module mega-menu) → Solutions (by size + industry + framework) → Customers → Pricing → Trust Center → Resources/Blog → Company, with a persistent "Get a demo / Get started" CTA.

## 5. Synthesized patterns

### 5a. Recurring page architecture
Across every mature player, the nav converges on the same skeleton (order varies slightly):

1. **Home**
2. **Product / Platform / How-it-works** — often a mega-menu of modules; scribes show the pre/during/post encounter flow.
3. **Solutions — segmented** by audience (For Clinicians vs For Hospitals/Health Systems), by specialty/vertical, and (GRC) by framework.
4. **Customers / Case studies** — named logos + outcome metrics; increasingly its own top-level page.
5. **Security / Trust (Center)** — HIPAA/SOC 2 for scribes; a full self-serve Trust Center for GRC (and Heidi in the scribe world).
6. **Pricing** — present for PLG/SMB plays (Heidi, Freed, Prowler), absent (contact-sales) for enterprise.
7. **Resources / Blog** — plus ROI calculators, whitepapers, changelog, help center.
8. **About / Company** — mission, "built by clinicians," team, careers.
9. **Book a demo / Get started** — persistent CTA in the nav and repeated across the page.

### 5b. Homepage section pattern (the recurring spine)
`Hero → social proof (logos/vanity stat) → problem/agitation → how-it-works (the encounter flow) → outcomes/proof (named-customer metrics, KLAS, studies) → security/trust → [pricing for PLG] → final CTA → footer (compliance badges)`

Every scribe uses the **three-stage encounter flow** (pre / during-encounter / post) as its "how it works." Every GRC platform uses the **snapshot-vs-live-posture contrast** + framework grid.

### 5c. How the best players resolve clinical-value vs compliance/back-office (Salvia's exact tension)
- **The default winning move is clinical-lead, back-office-named-second.** Nabla, Suki, Freed, Heidi, DeepScribe all lead with clinician relief ("focus on the patient, not the screen") and treat coding/revenue/compliance as the second act.
- **Ambience is the proof that you can name compliance in the hero without losing the clinician** — its hero is "The AI Platform Clinicians Choose for Documentation **and Coding**," and the subhead adds "strengthen revenue integrity and **ensure compliance** so clinicians can focus on their best care." Compliance is framed as *a benefit that accrues automatically from good documentation*, not a chore the doctor must do. That's the exact reframe Salvia needs.
- **Commure is the cautionary tale** — leading with RCM/claims ("$25B+ Annual Claims") reads as a back-office/CFO product, not a doctor's tool. Salvia should not lead compliance/admin in the hero.
- **GRC players show how to make an always-on agent trustworthy:** personify it as a tireless colleague, give it a bounded job (draft / flag / complete), keep a human in the loop ("second set of eyes," "flag before it escalates"), and attach a hard acceptance/accuracy number. Never claim the agent acts unsupervised.

### 5d. India-market insight (the decisive GTM finding)
- **The India-native, compliance-native lane is open.** Augnito deliberately stripped India framing to chase global enterprise; EkaScribe owns the ABDM-native scribe position but sells a whole clinic OS, not a compliance product; NABH readiness is only ever a *badge* bolted onto HMIS suites — **no one sells a compliance-native "prove-your-NABH-from-the-clinical-record" product.** That is Salvia's whitespace.
- **India table-stakes trust badges are local:** ABDM/ABHA (M1/M2/M3), NHA-Approved, FHIR, NABH/NABL/JCI, DPDP — not HIPAA/SOC 2 (which read as "foreign" here). The site must carry the Indian badge set.
- **The India wedge features that buyers reward:** multilingual capture (15+ Indian languages), voice-to-prescription output, and ABHA/registry linkage. Salvia's clinical wedge should speak this language.
- **Pricing is hidden and reframed as affordability** industry-wide; **public INR tier cards are a genuine gap** Salvia could exploit for the clinic/SMB segment (Freed/Heidi/Prowler proved transparency wins SMB trust), while keeping hospital pricing sales-led.

**Strategic frame:** India-first, clinical wedge now, compliance vision layered. Two buyer motions on one site — clinic/SMB (self-serve-ish, price-transparent, PLG-flavored) and hospital (sales-led, compliance-and-scale story). Borrow the scribe homepage spine + the GRC "always-on agent" vision layer + the Indian badge set.

### 6b. Homepage nav (recommended)
`Product · For Clinics · For Hospitals · Compliance · Pricing · Resources · [Book a demo] / [Start free]`

### 6c. Homepage section-by-section skeleton
1. **Hero — clinical-lead, compliance-named.** Headline on the doctor's win (faster, better notes, more patient time); subhead names the payoff (audit-ready / NABH compliance that builds itself from your notes). Dual CTA. India badge strip (ABDM/DPDP-ready) directly under.
2. **Social proof.** Pilot clinic/hospital logos + one vanity stat (notes drafted / hours saved / languages supported). When pilots are pre-logo, use "in talks" carefully — never claim "signed."
3. **Problem / agitation.** The doctor's documentation burden AND the looming NABH/audit evidence scramble — one line each, so both personas see themselves.
4. **How it works — the encounter flow.** Voice note → SOAP + vitals → typed compliance widgets → policy check → **the compliance agent maps it to your framework**. This is where clinical wedge visibly becomes compliance vision.
5. **Outcomes / proof.** Named-customer metrics once available; interim: accuracy/recall, hours saved, languages. Structure every stat as "[X] per [source]."
6. **The compliance agent (vision layer).** Personify it — the always-on quality/compliance colleague that drafts, flags gaps *before the audit*, and maps every note to NABH/JCI/NSQHS/CMS. Human-in-loop framing ("you confirm; it never submits unsupervised"). Live posture/coverage score visual. One hard number.
7. **Frameworks grid.** "One capture engine, many standards" — NABH, JCI, NSQHS, CMS/HEDIS tiles; reinforces country-by-country.
8. **Security / Trust.** ABDM/ABHA, DPDP, FHIR, encryption, data handling. Link to full Compliance/Trust page.
9. **Pricing teaser.** "Transparent INR pricing for clinics · custom for hospitals" → Pricing page.
10. **Final CTA.** Dual: "Start free" / "Book a demo."
11. **Footer.** Compliance/badge row (ABDM · DPDP · FHIR · ISO/SOC2 as you earn them), sitemap, Resources.

### 6d. The hero fork — recommendation
**Lead clinical, name compliance in the subhead (the Ambience pattern), not the reverse.**

- **Why clinical-lead:** every winning scribe leads with clinician relief; the two counter-examples that lead back-office (Commure's RCM, and any pure compliance pitch) read as CFO/administrator tools, not doctors' tools — and Salvia's own wedge is that pilot doctors buy clinical value first. Leading compliance would sell to the buyer who isn't in the room during the pilot.
- **Why still name compliance in the hero:** Ambience proves you can put "and compliance" in the hero sentence without losing the clinician, *as long as it's framed as an automatic payoff of good documentation, not a chore*. This is Salvia's single differentiator and the reason it out-lives a commodity scribe — so it must appear above the fold, just not as the promise.
- **Concrete hero shape:** headline = the clinical win (better documentation, less time, more patient face-time, in your language); subhead = "…and every note quietly builds your NABH/audit-ready compliance record." Agent + frameworks get their own dedicated sections (6, 7) and the Compliance page — that's where the vision is made tangible and trustworthy, following the GRC playbook (personified always-on agent, live posture, human-in-loop, hard number).
